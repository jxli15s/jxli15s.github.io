Featured work

Learning across materials and scales.

These projects use data and learned models to reach structures, dynamics, and functions that direct first-principles calculations cannot access alone.

01 Nature Communications · 2024

Active learning reveals a layer-by-layer phase transition.

Reconstructive transitions require chemical bonds to break and reform, while direct first-principles molecular dynamics cannot reach the full length and time scales of the process.

On-the-fly active learning, a moment-tensor potential, and advanced sampling enabled 165,888-atom trajectories. They show the β→λ transition in Ti3O5 nucleating in the ab plane and advancing layer by layer along c.

  • Active learning
  • ML potential
  • Large-scale MD

02 Communications Physics · 2024

Transfer learning reaches realistic moiré scales.

Transfer learning adapts a DeePMD neural-network potential trained on untwisted MoTe2 to relax moiré supercells down to a 1.1° twist angle, extending beyond the 2.88° limit reached with large-scale plane-wave DFT.

The relaxed structures bridge atomic reconstruction, first-principles electronic bands, and a continuum model, revealing robust Chern bands and strong strain-induced gauge fields across experimentally relevant twist angles.

  • Transfer learning
  • Twisted MoTe2
  • Moiré relaxation

03 Advanced Materials · 2023

One device, two modes of visual intelligence.

A bias-switchable architecture converts a single device between fast photodetection and neuromorphic vision sensing. The same platform can acquire optical information, retain it, and perform early-stage preprocessing.

This dual-mode behavior offers a compact route from sensing to memory and neural-network-ready data without separating each function into a different component.

  • Neuromorphic vision
  • Photodetection
  • In-sensor computing

04 The European Physical Journal Plus · 2023

Symbolic regression makes materials models interpretable.

Interpretable symbolic regression yields compact, transferable models of Vickers hardness and fracture toughness using only the bulk and shear moduli as inputs.

Coupled with machine-learned elastic moduli, these equations create an efficient high-throughput route to screen for materials that combine superhardness with fracture resistance.

  • Symbolic regression
  • Interpretable ML
  • Mechanical properties

05 Nature Communications · 2021

Computation becomes a reusable discovery platform.

A high-throughput workflow combined phonon data, automated stability filters, band-crossing searches, and topological classification across more than 10,000 materials.

The resulting database demonstrates an essential ML foundation: turn domain knowledge into structured, reproducible data that can guide the next calculation and the next experiment.
